# Preliminary work: suggested NSF wording

We implemented a pilot-assisted bosonic displacement-recovery simulation using an unknown qubit encoded in the first two Fock levels, independent coherent probes, and unconditional entanglement fidelity. In 560,000 held-out trials across seven noise conditions and twenty independently calibrated seeds, a reference-distance regularizer improved fidelity over probe-likelihood ML at a designated matched-noise condition (Fe 0.95212 versus 0.88792; paired difference 0.06419, 95% CI [0.06358,0.06476]). A prior-matched conventional Gaussian posterior produced identical estimates, and a prior-mean mismatch reversed the gain. These results demonstrate a reproducible quantum-recovery evaluation pipeline and identify ordinary prior information and calibration sensitivity as essential controls. They do not demonstrate a distinct geometric decoder advantage or protecting-code performance. The proposed work extends this infrastructure to genuine finite-energy bosonic syndrome recovery with stronger baselines and resource accounting.

Use only with the benchmark report and its explicit limitations. This package is local preliminary computational work, not a published result.
