# A five-minute guided demonstration

**0:00–1:00 — Quantum mechanics.** Begin Exploration. Watch separated dots build a pattern. Widen the slit and increase the separation independently. Ask: what happens to the broad envelope, and what happens to the fine fringes? Open “Explain Like I'm 12.”

**1:00–2:00 — Quantum field theory.** Enable the path marker and raise the information setting. Explain that interactions can create a path record; no conscious observer is required. The background field texture is an analogy. The screen calculation stays the same.

**2:00–3:00 — GWFM.** Change the proposed path count, spread, and overlap. Show that these drawing controls leave detector statistics unchanged. Ask what mathematical law would turn the interpretation into a predictive model.

**3:00–4:00 — SEFI and DEFI.** Select the four identity layers. In DEFI, move geometry drive and the illustrative coupling. These are a way of organizing questions about identity and interaction, not established microscopic properties.

**4:00–5:00 — Compare and question.** Move the shared observation control and read the five simultaneous explanations. Open the Advanced Research View only if the audience wants equations. End with the open questions about geometry, particles, and matter.

## Language rules

Use “the calculation predicts” for the standard model under its assumptions; “the illustration shows” for visual analogies; and “the proposal asks” for GWFM, SEFI, and DEFI. Do not call a displayed curve an observed worldline, a classical pattern a particle, or a coupling animation a proven field law.

The four SEFI labels are simplified for this educational experience. Their descriptions should be reviewed against the author's latest manuscripts before claiming that this app is a definitive statement of the framework. Future revisions should cite a specific manuscript version when formal definitions are adopted.
