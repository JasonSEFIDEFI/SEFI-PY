# Quantum reference and interpretive overlays

## Reference calculation

For two equally illuminated rectangular slits in the scalar far-field approximation, the screen intensity is proportional to

```text
I(y) = sinc²(π a sin(θ)/λ) × [1 + V cos(2π d sin(θ)/λ)]
θ = arctan(y/L)
sinc(x) = sin(x)/x, sinc(0) = 1
```

`a` is the slit width (10–80 micrometres), `d` is the separation (120–400 micrometres), `λ = 550 nm`, and `L = 1 m`. This is a far-field educational approximation; using the exact geometric angle does not make it an exact near-field diffraction solution.

The finite screen covers −30 to +30 mm in 401 uniformly spaced sample bins. Values are normalized over that window, so the resulting probabilities are conditional on arrival within the displayed window. They are not total fluxes or detection efficiencies.

## Observation control

Let `p` be the slider value divided by 100 and `g` the marker interaction setting. The demonstration chooses `D = gp` when the marker is enabled and `D = 0` otherwise. This mapping is a pedagogical parameterization, not an experimental calibration or a calculation of information in bits.

For an ideal pure path-marker state in the balanced two-path setting, the app takes `V = sqrt(1 − D²)`. More generally the distinguishability–visibility relation is a bound, `D² + V² ≤ 1`; other noise and mixed states can reduce visibility further. See [Englert (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.2154).

At D=0 the interference term has full contrast. At D=1 that term vanishes, leaving the single-slit envelope. The remaining far-field distribution is not arbitrarily replaced with two classical peaks. A which-path record can be generated by correlations without absorbing the particle, although some actual detectors do absorb it.

## Individual detections

The animation samples bins using the reference cumulative distribution and a deterministic pseudo-random generator. Dots do not simulate an individual particle's microscopic trajectory. Their transverse jitter is only a drawing offset. Each physical parameter change or Clear screen restarts the seed and sample. A chapter switch or display-only geometry control retains the same detections.

The animation is capped at 6,000 dots. This is a visual performance limit, not a prediction of event rate. Simulated events per second are also an animation choice.

## What the other chapters do

The QFT view is an analogy grounded in the established field description; it does not solve a field Hamiltonian or detector dynamics. It shares the same quantum reference distribution.

GWFM path bundles, overlap and narrowing are proposed explanatory graphics. Their count, spread, and opacity do not define a worldline measure or an amplitude law. SEFI's four identity layers are interpretive categories in this experience. DEFI's geometry–identity response is a dimensionless illustrative coupling, not an equation derived from a physical action.

No proposed overlay independently derives the Born rule, matter properties, or spacetime geometry. A future predictive extension must specify fields, states, observables, dynamics, boundary conditions, conservation laws, parameter units, and a way to compare independent predictions with experiment.

## Omitted physics

No microscopic detector model, near-field propagation solver, finite detector point-spread function, background counts, thermal decoherence, unequal slit illumination, extra slit phase, quantum many-body effects, or numerical QFT calculation. Apparatus graphics are schematic and not to scale. This is a teaching instrument and proposed-interpretation explorer, not an experimental simulator calibrated to a specific instrument.
