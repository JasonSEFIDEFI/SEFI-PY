# Publication readiness: one bounded paper

25 October 2026. Working assessment / Not Promoted. This is not an approved manuscript or submission.

## Candidate scope

Provisional title: **Binding and constrained energy structure of localized states in a two-complex-scalar model**.

The paper should state the action and assumed background, derive the vacuum and analytic separator, construct and continue the spherical branch, resolve its free-threshold crossing, distinguish localized stationary states above threshold, and report numerical and variational checks.

The present calculations materially improve the evidence beyond one selected profile. They do not yet establish originality sufficient for publication.

## What is now supported

- Reproduction of the archived reference with three-grid continuation across thirteen charge samples.
- Selected two-daughter energetic comparisons.
- A numerically resolved free-threshold crossing near Q=1225.43, cross-checked through continuum collocation.
- Stationary localized solutions above that threshold at selected smaller charges.
- Consistency with the variational charge first law.
- A conditional analytic ground-state representation for phase-sector energy forms and selected finite-grid Hessian checks.

All quantities are dimensionless, classical and specific to the displayed potential.

## What remains before submission

1. Establish precise overlap with existing multi-field Q-ball/Friedberg-Lee-Sirlin work. A branch threshold by itself is not a novelty claim.
2. Map possible folds with frequency or pseudo-arclength continuation. Fixed-Q relaxation failure near Q=900 is not nonexistence.
3. Extend the coupling study toward lambda=16 and derive or test an asymptotic scaling prediction. The separator alone does not supply the branch diagram.
4. Separate symmetry modes, extended vacuum modes and localized modes using domain/refinement checks.
5. Test selected charge-preserving nonradial physical-time perturbations with domain/time-step checks and energy/charge accounting, if the manuscript claims dynamical stability.
6. Obtain independent technical review of the action normalization, Hessian, solver, interpretation and novelty.
7. Review the final manuscript for consistency with every plotted/quoted result and all existing submission histories.

A narrower stationary-branch paper need not prove general nonlinear stability, but must state that limit explicitly and demonstrate a distinct contribution.

Whether these features yield an original mathematical or physical contribution requires a term-by-term comparison; field counting alone is insufficient.
